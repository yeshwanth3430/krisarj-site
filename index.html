Stage 1 · Linear Algebra In progress

What you learn: vectors, matrices, elimination, vector spaces, orthogonality, determinants, eigenvalues and the SVD, in 15 topics.

Why it matters in markets

A portfolio is a vector of positions and a set of products is a matrix of payoffs. Building a payoff, finding the best hedge, measuring beta, splitting risk into factors: all of it is linear algebra.

Stage 2 · Single Variable Calculus Later

What you learn: Limits, derivatives, integrals, series and Taylor expansions.

Why it matters in markets

Every change in a price is a derivative: an option's delta is how fast its premium moves when Nifty moves, and gamma is how fast delta itself moves. Taylor expansions are how traders approximate P&L for a small move.

After this stage you can:

  • read delta and gamma as derivatives
  • approximate an option's P&L for a 50-point Nifty move
  • understand continuous compounding and discounting

Stage 3 · Multivariable Calculus Later

What you learn: Partial derivatives, gradients, Lagrange multipliers, multiple integrals, vector calculus.

Why it matters in markets

A premium depends on many things at once: spot, volatility, time and rates. Partial derivatives give each Greek separately; Lagrange multipliers solve 'best portfolio under a budget' problems.

After this stage you can:

  • compute every Greek as a partial derivative
  • optimise a portfolio with constraints
  • see risk as a surface, not a single number

Stage 4 · Probability Later

What you learn: Random variables, distributions, expectation, conditioning, limit theorems.

Why it matters in markets

Prices are uncertain. Probability turns that uncertainty into expected payoffs, the chance an option expires in the money, value at risk, and the bell curve behind most pricing models.

After this stage you can:

  • price a payoff as an expected value
  • estimate the chance a strike is crossed by expiry
  • understand why returns are modelled as normal (and where that fails)

Stage 5 · Differential Equations Later

What you learn: First and second order ODEs, systems, Laplace transforms, the road to PDEs.

Why it matters in markets

How a quantity changes over time: mean-reverting spreads, interest-rate models, and finally the partial differential equation that an option price must satisfy.

After this stage you can:

  • solve a mean-reversion model and read its half-life
  • understand time decay as a differential equation
  • be ready for the Black–Scholes PDE

Stage 6 · Mathematics with Applications in Finance Later

What you learn: Stochastic processes, Itô calculus, Black–Scholes, portfolio theory, time series.

Why it matters in markets

Everything comes together: random price paths, the rules of calculus for them, the Black–Scholes price and Greeks, portfolio optimisation and forecasting from data.

After this stage you can:

  • derive and use Black–Scholes for Nifty options
  • build and rebalance an optimal portfolio
  • model and test a time series of prices

About

Most quant material starts at Black–Scholes. This series starts underneath it: the maths every model is built from, one idea at a time, tied to the Indian options market (Nifty CE/PE, strikes, lots).

Each lesson has two tabs: Notes (the idea explained from zero, every word defined) and Examples (an interactive lab where you type your own Nifty inputs and see every step, then worked examples and practice problems with hidden answers).
