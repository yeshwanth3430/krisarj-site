Linear Algebra · Lesson 4

Factorization into A = LU

Record elimination once, then solve for any new target in two quick passes.

Shown on a real money question: the same funds, many clients.

L from the multipliersFactor once, solve manyMIT 18.06, Lecture 4
The problem

Same funds, new targets every day

Nifty, bond and midcap funds; rules for budget, return and market exposure. The matrix $A$ never changes:

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 7 & 15 \\ 1 & 0 & 1.2 \end{bmatrix} $$

But each client brings a different target $b$: ₹10 lakh at 11.1%, ₹10 lakh at 9.8%, ₹15 lakh...

The idea

Don't redo elimination for every client. Save it as $A = LU$.

One tool first

Undo in reverse order

$$ (AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1} $$

Check: $AB\,B^{-1}A^{-1} = A\,I\,A^{-1} = I$.

Shoes and socks

Socks on, then shoes. To undo: shoes off first, then socks.

Elimination applied $E_{21}$, $E_{31}$, $E_{32}$. To undo it: $E_{32}^{-1}$ first, $E_{21}^{-1}$ last.

2 × 2

From EA = U to A = LU

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -12 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 7 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -5 \end{bmatrix} $$

Move $E$ to the other side (its inverse adds back 12 × row 1):

$$ \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 7 \end{bmatrix}}_{A} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 12 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{L}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -5 \end{bmatrix}}_{U} $$

Lower triangular with the multiplier; Upper triangular with the pivots.

3 × 3

The multipliers go straight into L

$$ \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 7 & 15 \\ 1 & 0 & 1.2 \end{bmatrix}}_{A} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ \mathbf{12} & 1 & 0 \\ \mathbf{1} & \mathbf{0.2} & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{L}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -5 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & -0.4 \end{bmatrix}}_{U} $$
Key fact

No row exchanges ⇒ the multipliers $\ell_{21} = 12$, $\ell_{31} = 1$, $\ell_{32} = 0.2$ sit directly in $L$. No extra work.

Why not E?

E is messy, L is clean

$$ E = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -12 & 1 & 0 \\ \mathbf{1.4} & -0.2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \qquad L = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 12 & 1 & 0 \\ \mathbf{1} & 0.2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Going forward, steps interfere: removing 0.2 × the new row 2 quietly adds back $0.2 \times 12 = 2.4$ of row 1, so $-1 + 2.4 = 1.4$.

Going backward with $L$, nothing interferes. That's why software stores $L$ and $U$.

Solving

Two triangular passes

Forward: $Lc = b$, top down.   Back: $Ux = c$, bottom up.

Client: $b = (10, 98, 5.2)$

  • $c_1 = 10$, $c_2 = 98 - 120 = -22$, $c_3 = 5.2 - 10 + 4.4 = -0.4$
  • $z = 1$, then $-5y + 3 = -22 \Rightarrow y = 5$, then $x = 4$

₹4 lakh Nifty, ₹5 lakh bonds, ₹1 lakh midcap. No new elimination needed.

The cost

Factor once, solve many

Factoring: about $\tfrac13 n^3$ operations. Each new target: about $n^2$. For 1,000 assets: 333 million once, then 1 million per client.

A variation

A = LDU

Pull the pivots out of $U$ into a diagonal matrix $D$:

$$ U = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -0.4 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -0.6 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Now both triangular factors have 1s on the diagonal, and the pivots sit in the middle.

Row exchanges

PA = LU

A credit fund (12%, like Nifty) puts a 0 in a pivot position. Swap rows first, then factor:

$$ P_{23}A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 12 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 0.2 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix} $$
Permutations

$n!$ of them (6 for 3 × 3). Closed under multiplication. $P^{-1} = P^T$.

Watch out

Singular matrices can have LU too

The balanced fund (half Nifty, half bonds) factors with the same $L$:

$$ U = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -5 & -2.5 \\ 0 & 0 & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix} $$

The trouble shows up on $U$'s diagonal: a 0 pivot, so back substitution fails.

Test

All pivots on $U$'s diagonal non-zero ⇔ invertible.

Recap

Key takeaways

  1. $(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1}$: reverse order.
  2. $A = LU$: multipliers in $L$, pivots in $U$.
  3. $L$ is clean; $E$ is messy.
  4. Solve: forward $Lc = b$, back $Ux = c$.
  5. $n^3/3$ to factor, $n^2$ per target.
  6. Row swaps: $PA = LU$.
