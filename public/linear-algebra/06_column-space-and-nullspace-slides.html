Column Space and Nullspace

Two questions every matrix answers: which targets can I reach? and which trades change nothing?

With portfolios, hedges and put–call parity.

The setup

Funds as columns

Rows: budget (₹ lakh), return (₹ thousand), exposure. Columns: Nifty, bonds, and a balanced fund (half Nifty, half bonds).

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 7 & 9.5 \\ 1 & 0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} $$

The column space

Ax = b is solvable exactly when b ∈ C(A)

$Ax$ is always a mix of the columns:

$$ A\begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 3 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = 7\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 12 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + 3\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 7 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + 0\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 9.5 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ 105 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix} $$

So a target can be hit only if it's a mix of the columns. Balanced = ½ Nifty + ½ bonds adds no new direction: $C(A)$ stays the plane $R = 7B + 5E$.

The nullspace, 2 × 2

Twin index funds

Two Nifty funds from different houses: both columns $(1, 12)$.

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 12 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - 1 \\ 12 - 12 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Move ₹1 lakh from one twin to the other: nothing changes. All multiples $c(1, -1)$ form the nullspace, a line.

The nullspace, 3 × 3

Find it by elimination

$$ U = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -5 & -2.5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

No pivot in column 3, so balanced is free. Choose $z = -2$: then $y = 1$, $x = 1$.

Check: $1 + 1 - 2 = 0$, $\;12 + 7 - 19 = 0$, $\;1 + 0 - 1 = 0$ ✓

Meaning

Buy ₹1L Nifty + ₹1L bonds, sell ₹2L balanced: you built the balanced fund yourself.

A subspace

Do-nothing + do-nothing = do-nothing

If $Av = 0$ and $Aw = 0$:

$$ A(v + w) = 0 + 0 = 0 \qquad A(cv) = c \cdot 0 = 0 $$

So $N(A)$ is always a subspace, of the trade space $\mathbb{R}^n$. $C(A)$ is a subspace of the target space $\mathbb{R}^m$.

Swap balanced for midcap and elimination finds 3 pivots: $N(A) = \{0\}$, and every target has exactly one portfolio.

Every solution

One answer + the nullspace

$$ x = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 3 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} $$

$(7, 3, 0)$, $(6, 2, 2)$, $(4, 0, 6)$ all give ₹10L, ₹105K, exposure 7.

Not a subspace

The solutions are a shifted line

$(0, 0, 0)$ isn't a solution: doing nothing has budget 0, not 10.

Add two solutions: $(7, 3, 0) + (6, 2, 2) = (13, 5, 2)$ hits $(20, 210, 14)$, which is $2b$, not $b$.

Rule

Linear rules "$= 0$" give a subspace (a nullspace). A non-zero right side shifts it off the origin.

In markets

Put–call parity is a nullspace

Payoff per lot at 19,800 / 20,000 / 20,200: futures $(-200, 0, 200)$, 20,000 call $(0, 0, 200)$, 20,000 put $(200, 0, 0)$.

$$ 1\cdot\text{fut} - 1\cdot\text{call} + 1\cdot\text{put} = (0, 0, 0) $$

So "1 future + 2 calls" and "3 futures + 2 puts" pay the same everywhere. If one costs more, sell it and buy the other.

Recap

Key takeaways

  1. $Ax = b$ solvable ⇔ $b \in C(A)$.
  2. $N(A)$ = all $x$ with $Ax = 0$: do-nothing trades.
  3. Free columns in elimination ⇒ non-zero nullspace.
  4. $N(A) = \{0\}$ ⇒ one portfolio per target.
  5. All solutions = $x_p$ + nullspace.
  6. Solutions of $Ax = b \ne 0$ are not a subspace.
