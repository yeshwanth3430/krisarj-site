Linear Algebra · Lesson 1

The Geometry of Linear Equations

One question, three ways to see it: the row picture, the column picture and the matrix form $Ax = b$.

Explained from zero on a real money question: splitting ₹10 lakh between two funds.

2 × 2 and 3 × 3Portfolio mixMIT 18.06, Lecture 1
The question

You have ₹10 lakh and two funds

FundReturn per yearOn ₹1 lakh
Nifty 50 index fund12%₹12 thousand
Government bond fund7%₹7 thousand

You want to invest all ₹10 lakh and earn ₹1,05,000 a year.

The question

How much goes into each fund?

Returns are illustrative round numbers, not forecasts.

Step 1

Turn it into equations

Let $x$ = ₹ lakh in Nifty and $y$ = ₹ lakh in bonds. Each requirement is one equation:

$$ \begin{aligned} x + y &= 10 &&\text{budget (₹ lakh)}\\ 12x + 7y &= 105 &&\text{return (₹ thousand)} \end{aligned} $$

Two equations that must both hold are a system. Each unknown is only multiplied by a number and added, so the equations are linear.

The fundamental problem of linear algebra

Solve $n$ linear equations in $n$ unknowns.

The big idea

Three ways to read the same equations

ViewYou look atThe question becomes
Row pictureone equation at a timeWhere do the lines meet?
Column picture ★one fund at a timeWhich mix of fund arrows hits the target?
Matrix formeverything at onceFind $x$ with $Ax = b$.

The ★ marks the view the whole course is built on.

Step 2

Each fund is a vector

₹1 lakh of a fund uses ₹1 lakh of budget and earns its return. Stack the two numbers in a column:

$$ \text{Nifty} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix} \qquad \text{Bond} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix} $$
  • Scale: $7\,(1, 12) = (7, 84)$: ₹7 lakh of Nifty.
  • Add: $(1, 12) + (1, 7) = (2, 19)$: ₹1 lakh in each.
Linear combination

Scale vectors and add them: $x\,c + y\,d$. Here, a linear combination of the funds is simply a portfolio.

Step 3

Matrix form: $Ax = b$

$$ \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 7 \end{bmatrix}}_{A} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}}_{x} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ 105 \end{bmatrix}}_{b} $$

Rows of $A$ = the rules (budget, return). Columns of $A$ = the funds.

By columns: $A\begin{bmatrix}7\\3\end{bmatrix} = 7\begin{bmatrix}1\\12\end{bmatrix} + 3\begin{bmatrix}1\\7\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}10\\105\end{bmatrix}$

By rows: $\;1\cdot7 + 1\cdot3 = 10,\quad 12\cdot7 + 7\cdot3 = 105$

Key fact

$Ax$ is a combination of the columns of $A$.

View 1

Row picture: lines that cross

Each rule is a line. The answer is where they cross: ₹7 lakh Nifty, ₹3 lakh bonds.

Check: $7 + 3 = 10$ ✓ and $84 + 21 = 105$ ✓

View 2 ★

Column picture: mix the arrows

$$ x\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix} + y\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ 105 \end{bmatrix} $$

Same answer, seen as a mix of fund arrows landing exactly on the target $b$.

The bigger question

Which targets can we reach?

The span of some vectors = every point you can reach by mixing them.

Funds you may useSpanMeaning
Nifty onlya linealways exactly 12%
Bonds onlyanother linealways exactly 7%
Nifty + bondsthe whole planeany budget, any return
Column space $C(A)$

The span of the columns of $A$: every target your funds can produce.

Why it matters

$Ax = b$ has a solution exactly when $b$ is in $C(A)$.

Three funds: 3 × 3

Add a midcap fund and a third rule

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 7 & 15 \\ 1 & 0 & 1.2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ 111 \\ 7.4 \end{bmatrix} \quad \begin{matrix} \text{budget} \\ \text{return} \\ \text{market exposure} \end{matrix} $$

Row picture: three planes meet at one point: ₹5 lakh Nifty, ₹3 lakh bonds, ₹2 lakh midcap.

Three funds: 3 × 3

Column picture in 3D

$$ 5\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 12 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + 3\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 7 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + 2\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 15 \\ 1.2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ 111 \\ 7.4 \end{bmatrix} $$

Change the target and the row picture gets three new planes. The column picture keeps the same three arrows, just mixed differently.

When it goes wrong

Singular matrices

Two Nifty funds (both 12%) point the same way:

Singular vs invertible

Invertible: the columns fill the space: one solution for every $b$. Singular: a fund adds no new direction (a twin fund, or a 50/50 balanced fund): no solution or infinitely many.

Recap

Key takeaways

  1. A linear system is $Ax = b$: rows are rules, columns are funds.
  2. Row picture: lines (or planes) meet at the solution.
  3. Column picture ★: $b$ is a mix of the columns; the solution gives the amounts.
  4. $Ax$ is a combination of the columns of $A$.
  5. Every target is reachable exactly when the columns fill the space ($A$ invertible).
  6. A fund built from others adds no direction: $A$ is singular.
