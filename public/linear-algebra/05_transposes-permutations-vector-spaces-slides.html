Transposes, Permutations, Vector Spaces

Flip matrices, reorder rows, then step up from single vectors to whole spaces of vectors.

With portfolios: covariance, long-only funds and zero-cost trades.

Permutations

Same rules, different order

A permutation only re-lists the equations. Same facts, same answer.

$$ \begin{aligned} y &= 3 \\ x + y &= 8 \end{aligned} \quad\xrightarrow{\text{swap}}\quad \begin{aligned} x + y &= 8 \\ y &= 3 \end{aligned} $$

Left: the first rule has no $x$, a 0 where the pivot should be, so elimination is stuck. Right: already triangular: $y = 3$, $x = 5$.

Rules

$PA = LU$: reorder first, then eliminate. Undo a reorder with $P^T$: $P^TP = I$. 3 rules → 6 orders ($3!$).

Transposes

Rows become columns

$(A^T)_{ij} = A_{ji}$. In $A$ each column is a fund; in $A^T$ each row is a fund's profile:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 7 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}^T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 12 & 1 \\ 1 & 7 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Portfolio return as a row times a column:

$$ r^Tx = \begin{bmatrix} 12 & 7 & 15 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = 60 + 21 + 30 = 111 $$
A rule

(AB)ᵀ = BᵀAᵀ

Transpose each, reverse the order. For two clients:

$$ (AX)^T = \begin{bmatrix} 10 & 105 \\ 10 & 90 \end{bmatrix} = X^TA^T $$

$AX$ had one column per client; its transpose has one row per client. Same report, sorted the other way.

Symmetric matrices

RᵀR is always symmetric

$(R^TR)^T = R^T(R^T)^T = R^TR$. Scenario returns of Nifty and bonds:

$$ R = \begin{bmatrix} -10 & 4 \\ 10 & 7 \\ 30 & 10 \end{bmatrix} \;\Rightarrow\; R^TR = \begin{bmatrix} 1100 & 330 \\ 330 & 165 \end{bmatrix} $$

Entry $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 1)$ are the same dot product: Nifty · bonds $= -40 + 70 + 300$.

In markets

Why covariance is symmetric

Subtract the average returns (10% and 7%), then $\tfrac13 C^TC$:

$$ \text{Cov} = \begin{bmatrix} 266.7 & 40 \\ 40 & 6 \end{bmatrix} $$

A covariance matrix is $C^TC$ in disguise, so it is always symmetric: cov(Nifty, bonds) = cov(bonds, Nifty).

Vector spaces

Add and scale without leaving

A vector space is closed under adding and multiplying by any number, and contains $0$.

  • $(7, 3) + (4, 6) = (11, 9)$: merge two books
  • $-1 \times (7, 3) = (-7, -3)$: short the lot
  • $0 \times (7, 3) = (0, 0)$: the empty portfolio

All portfolios (long or short) form the space $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Not a space

Long-only portfolios fail

$x \ge 0, y \ge 0$: adding is fine, but $-1 \times (7, 3) = (-7, -3)$ escapes.

Fixed budget $x + y = 10$ fails too: $(7,3) + (6,4)$ has budget 20, and $0$ isn't in it.

Why it matters

A long-only constraint breaks linearity: portfolio construction needs inequality-constrained optimisation.

Subspaces

Zero-cost trades are a subspace

Trades whose changes add to zero: $x + y = 0$. Sums and multiples stay zero-cost, and $0$ is in it: a line through the origin.

Subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^3$: $\{0\}$, lines through 0, planes through 0, all of $\mathbb{R}^3$.

Zero-cost and zero-exposure: the line through $(-6, 1, 5)$, a trade that changes only the return (+10).

The column space

C(A): every target you can reach

All combinations of the columns. Nifty + bonds with three rules gives a plane in $\mathbb{R}^3$:

$(10, 105, 7)$ ✓ reachable; $(10, 105, 5)$ ✗: exposure always equals the Nifty amount.

Recap

Key takeaways

  1. $PA = LU$; $n!$ permutations; $P^{-1} = P^T$.
  2. $(A^T)_{ij} = A_{ji}$; $(AB)^T = B^TA^T$.
  3. $R^TR$ is symmetric: so is covariance.
  4. Spaces are closed under $+$ and scaling.
  5. Long-only isn't a space; zero-cost trades are.
  6. $C(A)$ = targets the columns can reach.
