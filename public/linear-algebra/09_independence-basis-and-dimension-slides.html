Independence, Basis and Dimension

Which funds are genuinely different? What's the smallest menu that still reaches everything?

How many independent bets does a menu really offer?

Independence

No fund can be rebuilt from the others

$c_1v_1 + \cdots + c_nv_n = 0$ only when every $c = 0$.

Dependent example:

$$ -\tfrac12\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 12 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} - \tfrac12\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 7 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 9.5 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix} = 0 $$

Balanced $= \tfrac12$ Nifty $+ \tfrac12$ bonds. Dependent = a do-nothing trade exists.

The test

Put them in columns, count pivots

Columns independent ⇔ $N(A) = \{0\}$ ⇔ no free variables ⇔ rank = n.

More vectors than entries ($n > m$) ⇒ always dependent.

Span and basis

Reach everything, waste nothing

Span: all combinations = every reachable target.

Basis: independent and spanning: the smallest complete menu.

Standard basis = Arrow–Debreu

$(1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0), (0, 0, 1)$: each pays 1 in exactly one scenario.

Nifty, bonds, midcap are another basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$.

Dimension

Every basis has the same size

That number is the dimension: the number of genuinely different directions.

  • $\mathbb{R}^3$: dimension 3
  • The plane $R = 7B + 5E$: dimension 2

Matrices have a rank; spaces have a dimension: $\text{rank}(A) = \dim C(A)$.

Bases of C(A) and N(A)

Pivot columns and special solutions

Basis of $C(A)$: the pivot columns of $A$. $\dim = r$.

Basis of $N(A)$: the special solutions. $\dim = n - r$.

Careful

Use $A$'s columns, not $R$'s: row operations change the column space.

Five funds

Only 3 real directions

$$ R = \begin{bmatrix} \boxed{1} & 1 & 0 & 0.5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \boxed{1} & 0.5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \boxed{1} \end{bmatrix} $$

Nifty, ETF, bonds, balanced, midcap → pivots: Nifty, bonds, midcap.

$\dim C(A) = 3$ (every target), $\dim N(A) = 2$ (ETF = Nifty; balanced = mix).

The count

n = r + (n − r)

Every fund is either a new direction (pivot) or a redundancy (free).

$$ 5 \text{ funds} = 3 \text{ directions} + 2 \text{ redundancies} $$
As a trader

50 products driven by 3 risk factors: at most 3 independent bets, at least 47 redundant routes.

In markets

A basis of payoffs

Futures, 20,000 call, 20,000 put, 19,900 call over 3 scenarios:

  • Basis of $C(A)$: futures, 20,000 call, 19,900 call (dim 3)
  • Basis of $N(A)$: $(1, -1, 1, 0)$, put–call parity (dim 1)

The put is redundant given futures and the call.

Recap

Key takeaways

  1. Independent ⇔ rank $= n$ ⇔ $N(A) = \{0\}$.
  2. Basis = independent + spans.
  3. Dimension = vectors in any basis.
  4. Basis of $C(A)$: pivot columns of $A$.
  5. Basis of $N(A)$: special solutions.
  6. $n = r + (n - r)$.
