Linear Algebra · Lesson 12

Graphs, Networks, Incidence Matrices

Turn a network of prices into a matrix. Loops become the left null space, and a loop that doesn't add to zero is an arbitrage.

nodes − edges + loops = 1Aᵀy = 0MIT 18.06, Lecture 12
The network

Contracts and spreads

Nodes: Nifty spot, Oct, Nov, Dec futures (20,000 / 20,100 / 20,250 / 20,400).

Edges: quoted spreads, e.g. Oct − spot = 100.

Incidence matrix: one row per edge, $-1$ at the start node, $+1$ at the end node. Then

$$ Ax = \text{the spread on every edge.} $$
The null space

Only relative prices matter

$Ax = 0$ ⇒ all prices equal: $N(A) = c(1, 1, 1, 1)$.

Add 50 points to every price and no spread changes.

Ground a node

Fix spot = 20,000 (the numeraire) and the spreads decide every other price. Rank = $n - 1$.

Loops

Positions that cancel

Hold $y_i$ lots of spread $i$. Then $A^Ty$ = net position in each contract.

$A^Ty = 0$ (Kirchhoff's law): flat in every contract. Its solutions are loops:

$$ y = (1, 1, -1, 0, 0) $$

buy spot→Oct, buy Oct→Nov, sell spot→Nov: net zero in every contract.

Counting

Euler's formula

Loops $= \dim N(A^T) = m - (n - 1)$. So

$$ \text{nodes} - \text{edges} + \text{loops} = 1 $$

4 contracts, 5 spreads: $4 - 5 + 2 = 1$ ✓. Two independent loops.

The test

No arbitrage = every loop sums to 0

Quotes come from real prices ⇔ $e \in C(A)$ ⇔ $y^Te = 0$ for every loop (Lesson 10's test).

Consistent: $100 + 150 - 250 = 0$ ✓

Mispriced spot→Nov at 240: $100 + 150 - 240 = 10$ ✗

The trade

Trade the loop

Buy the direct spot→Nov spread at 240, sell spot→Oct and Oct→Nov (worth 250).

Result

Net 0 lots in every contract ($A^Ty = 0$), 10 points locked in.

Currency triangles work the same way with logs: $83 \times 1.08 = 89.64$, so a ₹90/€ quote is 0.4% off.
