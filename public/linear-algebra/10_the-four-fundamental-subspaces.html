The Four Fundamental Subspaces

Every matrix carries four spaces: the targets you can reach, the trades that do nothing, everything you can measure, and the hidden link between your rules. One row reduction finds all four.

Part 3 Explanation

The funds used in the examples

Columns (what ₹1 lakh does to budget, return, exposure): Nifty $(1, 12, 1)$, Nifty ETF $(1, 12, 1)$, bonds $(1, 7, 0)$, balanced $(1, 9.5, 0.5)$, midcap $(1, 15, 1.2)$. Rows are the rules: budget, return, exposure. So $A$ is $m \times n$ with $m$ = number of rules, $n$ = number of funds.

1. Conditions and important concepts

1. The four subspaces

Any $m \times n$ matrix $A$ gives four subspaces. Two you already know; two are new:

Definition · The four fundamental subspaces
SubspaceMade ofLives inIn money terms
Column space $C(A)$all combinations of the columns$\mathbb{R}^m$targets you can reach
Null space $N(A)$all $x$ with $Ax = 0$$\mathbb{R}^n$trades that do nothing
Row space $C(A^T)$all combinations of the rows$\mathbb{R}^n$everything you can measure about a trade
Left null space $N(A^T)$all $y$ with $A^Ty = 0$$\mathbb{R}^m$combinations of rules that are always zero: a hidden link

2. Column space C(A) (known)

All mixes of the funds' columns: the targets $b$ for which $Ax = b$ can be solved. Basis: the pivot columns of $A$.

3. Null space N(A) (known)

All trades $x$ with $Ax = 0$: they change no rule. Basis: the special solutions.

4. Row space C(Aᵀ) (new)

All combinations of the rows of $A$. A row is a rule; a combination of rows is a new rule, like "return $- 12 \times$ budget". So:

$$ \boxed{\text{Row space} = \text{every measurement you can build from your rules.}} $$

The rows of $A$ are the columns of $A^T$, so the row space is $C(A^T)$.

5. Left null space N(Aᵀ) (new)

All $y$ with $A^Ty = 0$. Taking the transpose of both sides, that is $y^TA = 0$: a row vector $y^T$ on the left of $A$ giving zero, hence the name. In words: a combination of the rules (weights $y$ on the rows) that comes out to zero for every fund.

$$ \boxed{\text{Left null space} = \text{combinations of rules that are always zero: a hidden link between the rules.}} $$

6. Where they live

$N(A)$ and $C(A^T)$ are made of vectors with $n$ entries (one per fund): they live in $\mathbb{R}^n$. $C(A)$ and $N(A^T)$ are made of vectors with $m$ entries (one per rule): they live in $\mathbb{R}^m$.

7. Dimensions

Rule · Dimensions of the four subspaces

For an $m \times n$ matrix of rank $r$:

$$ \dim C(A) = r \qquad \dim N(A) = n - r \qquad \dim C(A^T) = r \qquad \dim N(A^T) = m - r $$

8. Row rank = column rank

The row space and the column space have the same dimension $r$, even though they live in different places. Reason: every pivot sits in one pivot row and one pivot column, so both are counted by the same pivots.

9. Basis of the row space: the rows of R

Method · Row space basis = the non-zero rows of R

Row operations replace rows by combinations of rows, and can be undone. So $A$ and $R$ have the same row space, and the first $r$ (non-zero) rows of $R$ are a basis.

Important

For the row space you may use $R$'s rows. For the column space you must use $A$'s own pivot columns (Lesson 9). Row operations keep the row space but change the column space.

10. Finding the left null space: E

Method · Reduce [A | I] to [R | E]

Put the identity next to $A$ and row reduce the whole thing. The right block becomes the matrix $E$ that records every row operation, so $EA = R$. (If $A$ is square and invertible, $R = I$ and $E = A^{-1}$, as in Gauss–Jordan.)

Method · Left null space basis = the rows of E next to the zero rows of R

If row $i$ of $R$ is all zeros, then (row $i$ of $E$) $\times A$ = row $i$ of $R$ = 0. So the last $m - r$ rows of $E$ are a basis of $N(A^T)$.

11. What the left null space tells you: which targets are reachable

If $y^TA = 0$, then for any portfolio $x$: $y^T(Ax) = (y^TA)x = 0$. So every reachable target $b = Ax$ must have $y^Tb = 0$. And that is also enough:

Rule · The left null space test

$Ax = b$ is solvable $\iff$ $y^Tb = 0$ for every $y$ in $N(A^T)$. This is Lesson 8's zero-row test, written as a vector.

12. The two pairs

The dimensions add up in pairs:

$$ \boxed{\dim C(A^T) + \dim N(A) = r + (n - r) = n} \qquad \boxed{\dim C(A) + \dim N(A^T) = r + (m - r) = m} $$

13. Square and invertible

If $A$ is $n \times n$ with rank $n$: $C(A) = C(A^T) = \mathbb{R}^n$, and $N(A) = N(A^T) = \{0\}$. No do-nothing trade, no hidden link, every target reachable in one way.

2. Procedure: all four in one go

StepWhat to doGives
1Write $[A \mid I]$ ($I$ is $m \times m$).

3. 2 × 2 example

Nifty and its twin ETF, with the budget and return rules:

$$ [\,A \mid I\,] = \left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 12 & 12 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right] \;\xrightarrow{\text{row 2} - 12 \times \text{row 1}}\; \left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -12 & 1 \end{array}\right] = [\,R \mid E\,] $$

(Row 2: $12 - 12 = 0$, $12 - 12 = 0$, $0 - 12(1) = -12$, $1 - 12(0) = 1$.) One pivot: $r = 1$, $m = n = 2$.

SubspaceBasisDimMeaning
$C(A)$$(1, 12)$ (column 1 of $A$)1only targets with return $= 12 \times$ budget
$N(A)$$(-1, 1)$1swap Nifty for its twin
$C(A^T)$$(1, 1)$ (row 1 of $R$)1the only real measurement: total Nifty held, $x_1 + x_2$
$N(A^T)$$(-12, 1)$ (row 2 of $E$)1the hidden link: $-12 \cdot \text{budget} + \text{return} = 0$

Check the left null vector: $y^TA = (-12, 1)\left[\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 12 \end{smallmatrix}\right] = (-12 + 12,\ -12 + 12) = (0, 0)$ ✓. So every reachable target has return $= 12 \times$ budget: $(10, 120)$ passes ($-120 + 120 = 0$), $(10, 105)$ fails ($-120 + 105 = -15$), exactly Lesson 8's answer.

Check the pairs: $1 + 1 = 2 = n$ ✓, $1 + 1 = 2 = m$ ✓.

4. 3 × 3 example

Nifty, bonds, balanced, all three rules:

$$ [\,A \mid I\,] = \left[\begin{array}{ccc|ccc} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 12 & 7 & 9.5 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0.5 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right] $$

The same five row operations as Lesson 7. The $A$ part changes exactly as before; here is what happens to the identity part:

Reading the row space

The two rows of $R$ are two measurements of a trade: how much Nifty it really holds (Nifty plus half the balanced) and how much bonds it really holds. Every rule is a combination of them:

  • Budget $(1, 1, 1) = 1 \cdot (1, 0, 0.5) + 1 \cdot (0, 1, 0.5)$ ✓ ($0.5 + 0.5 = 1$).
  • Return $(12, 7, 9.5) = 12 \cdot (1, 0, 0.5) + 7 \cdot (0, 1, 0.5)$ ✓ ($6 + 3.5 = 9.5$).
  • Exposure $(1, 0, 0.5) = 1 \cdot (1, 0, 0.5)$ ✓.

Three rules, but only two independent measurements: that's $\dim C(A^T) = 2$.

Reading the left null space

Check $y = (7, -1, 5)$ against every fund (each column of $A$):

  • Nifty: $7(1) - 1(12) + 5(1) = 7 - 12 + 5 = 0$ ✓
  • Bonds: $7(1) - 1(7) + 5(0) = 0$ ✓
  • Balanced: $7(1) - 1(9.5) + 5(0.5) = 7 - 9.5 + 2.5 = 0$ ✓

So for every portfolio, $7 \cdot \text{budget} - \text{return} + 5 \cdot \text{exposure} = 0$, which is $R = 7B + 5E$. The left null space found the hidden link between the rules, and with it the test for targets:

  • $(10, 105, 7)$: $70 - 105 + 35 = 0$ ✓ reachable.
  • $(10, 105, 5)$: $70 - 105 + 25 = -10$ ✗ not reachable.

Check the pairs: $2 + 1 = 3 = n$ ✓, $2 + 1 = 3 = m$ ✓.

For the curious: and with midcap instead of balanced?

Nifty, bonds, midcap is invertible ($r = 3$), so $R = I$ and $E = A^{-1}$. $C(A) = C(A^T) = \mathbb{R}^3$ and $N(A) = N(A^T) = \{0\}$: no do-nothing trade, no hidden link, every target reachable exactly once.

5. Bigger example: four funds

Nifty, ETF, bonds, balanced: $m = 3$ rules, $n = 4$ funds. Row reduction uses the same five operations, so $E$ comes out the same as in the 3 × 3 example:

$$ [\,R \mid E\,] = \left[\begin{array}{cccc|ccc} \boxed{1} & 1 & 0 & 0.5 & -1.4 & 0.2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \boxed{1} & 0.5 & 2.4 & -0.2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1.4 & -0.2 & 1 \end{array}\right] \qquad r = 2 $$

Now all four subspaces are non-trivial:

SubspaceLives inBasisDim
$C(A)$$\mathbb{R}^3$Nifty $(1, 12, 1)$, bonds $(1, 7, 0)$$r = 2$
$N(A)$$\mathbb{R}^4$$(-1, 1, 0, 0)$, $(-0.5, 0, -0.5, 1)$$n - r = 2$
$C(A^T)$$\mathbb{R}^4$$(1, 1, 0, 0.5)$, $(0, 0, 1, 0.5)$$r = 2$
$N(A^T)$$\mathbb{R}^3$$(1.4, -0.2, 1)$$m - r = 1$

Meaning. The row space now measures "Nifty content" $x_1 + x_2 + 0.5x_4$ (Nifty, plus the ETF, plus half the balanced) and "bond content" $x_3 + 0.5x_4$. The do-nothing trades are exactly the trades that leave both measurements at 0: check $s_1 = (-1, 1, 0, 0)$: $-1 + 1 + 0 = 0$ and $0 + 0 = 0$ ✓; $s_2 = (-0.5, 0, -0.5, 1)$: $-0.5 + 0 + 0.5 = 0$ and $-0.5 + 0.5 = 0$ ✓. The left null vector checks out for the ETF too: $7(1) - 12 + 5(1) = 0$ ✓.

Check the pairs: $2 + 2 = 4 = n$ ✓, $2 + 1 = 3 = m$ ✓.

6. Connection: the big picture

$$ \underbrace{\mathbb{R}^n}_{\text{trades}} \;:\; \underbrace{C(A^T)}_{\dim r\ (\text{measured})} \;+\; \underbrace{N(A)}_{\dim n - r\ (\text{invisible})} \qquad\qquad \underbrace{\mathbb{R}^m}_{\text{targets}} \;:\; \underbrace{C(A)}_{\dim r\ (\text{reachable})} \;+\; \underbrace{N(A^T)}_{\dim m - r\ (\text{hidden links})} $$

On the trade side ($\mathbb{R}^n$): each direction is either something your rules can see (row space) or something they can't (null space). On the target side ($\mathbb{R}^m$): each direction is either reachable (column space) or blocked by a hidden link (left null space).

Menu$m$$n$$r$$\dim N(A)$$\dim N(A^T)$So

Nifty, ETF, bonds, balanced34221$R = 7B + 5E$ locked

Reading it as a trader

The left null space is the market's hidden constraint: with only Nifty-and-bond-like products, return and exposure can't be chosen separately. Adding a product with a genuinely new direction (midcap) kills the left null space and frees every target. A market where $N(A^T) = \{0\}$, i.e. the products' payoffs span every scenario, is what finance calls a complete market.

Pairs: $r + (n - r) = n$ and $r + (m - r) = m$. Find everything with one reduction: $[A \mid I] \to [R \mid E]$.

Check yourself (MIT problem 10.1): A is $m \times n$ of rank $r$, and some $Ax = b$ has no solution. Which inequalities must hold? Why does $A^Ty = 0$ have non-zero solutions?

$r \le m$ and $r \le n$ always. "Some $b$ unreachable" means $C(A) \ne \mathbb{R}^m$, so $r < m$. Then $\dim N(A^T) = m - r > 0$, so the left null space has non-zero vectors: the hidden link that blocks those targets.

Check yourself (MIT lecture): the four subspaces of $\left[\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right]$.

$R = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right]$, $E = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} -1 & 2 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right]$, $r = 2$. $C(A)$: columns 1, 2 of $A$ (dim 2). $N(A)$: $(-1, -1, 1, 0)$, $(-1, 0, 0, 1)$ (dim 2). $C(A^T)$: $(1, 0, 1, 1)$, $(0, 1, 1, 0)$ (dim 2). $N(A^T)$: $(-1, 0, 1)$ (dim 1): row 3 minus row 1 is zero, since rows 1 and 3 are equal.

Example 1: Hedging, an unhedgeable risk

Rows: (market, currency, rates). With only Nifty fut $(1, 0, 0)$ and IT fut $(1, 1, 0)$:

$$ [\,A \mid I\,] = \left[\begin{array}{cc|ccc} 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right] \;\xrightarrow{\text{row 1} - \text{row 2}}\; \left[\begin{array}{cc|ccc} 1 & 0 & 1 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right] $$
SubspaceBasisDimMeaning
$C(A)$$(1, 0, 0)$, $(1, 1, 0)$2exposures with rates $= 0$
$N(A)$none0no redundant hedge
$C(A^T)$$(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$2both amounts can be measured
$N(A^T)$$(0, 0, 1)$1"rates exposure" is always 0

The left null vector says it plainly: no mix of these hedges ever touches rates. A target with rates exposure 2 fails the test ($0 \cdot 10 + 0 \cdot 3 + 1 \cdot 2 = 2 \ne 0$). Add Bank Nifty futures $(1, 0, 1)$ and the left null space disappears ($r = 3 = m$): every exposure becomes hedgeable. With all four hedges (adding the spread), $r = 3$ still, $N(A^T) = \{0\}$ and $N(A)$ has dimension 1: $(1, -1, 0, 1)$.

Example 2: Lot sizes, a scenario you can't touch

Rows: Nifty ends at 19,800 / 20,000 / 20,200 (points per lot). With only futures $(-200, 0, 200)$ and the 20,000 call $(0, 0, 200)$:

SubspaceBasisDimMeaning
$C(A)$$(-200, 0, 200)$, $(0, 0, 200)$2payoffs that are 0 at 20,000
$N(A)$none0no redundant product
$C(A^T)$$(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$2both lot counts can be measured
$N(A^T)$$(0, 1, 0)$1"payoff at 20,000" is always 0

Check the left null vector: $(0, 1, 0) \cdot (-200, 0, 200) = 0$ ✓ and $(0, 1, 0) \cdot (0, 0, 200) = 0$ ✓. Neither product pays at exactly 20,000, so a client payoff $(0, 100, 0)$ is unreachable. Adding the 19,900 call $(0, 100, 300)$ removes the hidden link: the market for these three scenarios becomes complete.
