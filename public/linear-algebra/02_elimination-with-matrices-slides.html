Elimination with Matrices

How every computer solves $Ax = b$: eliminate down to a triangle, then back-substitute.

Shown on a real money question: splitting ₹10 lakh across funds.

Pivots & multipliersPortfolio mixMIT 18.06, Lecture 2
The problem

Pictures don't scale

In Lesson 1 we solved a portfolio by drawing lines and spotting tricks. With 50 funds and 50 rules, nobody can draw or guess.

The same three-fund system, now solved properly:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 7 & 15 \\ 1 & 0 & 1.2 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ 111 \\ 7.4 \end{bmatrix} \;\; \begin{matrix} \text{budget} \\ \text{return} \\ \text{exposure} \end{matrix} $$
The method

Elimination: the way every software package solves equations.

The idea

Triangles are easy

$$ \begin{aligned} x + y + z &= 10 \\ -5y + 3z &= -9 \\ -0.4z &= -0.8 \end{aligned} \;\Rightarrow\; z = 2,\ y = 3,\ x = 5 $$

Solve the last equation, then work upwards. Elimination turns any good system into a triangle like this, without changing its solution.

Two words

Pivot: the diagonal entry used to clear the entries below it.
Multiplier: entry to clear ÷ pivot.

2 × 2

Two funds, one step

$$ \left[\begin{array}{cc|c} \boxed{1} & 1 & 10 \\ 12 & 7 & 105 \end{array}\right] \xrightarrow{\;R_2 - 12R_1\;} \left[\begin{array}{cc|c} \boxed{1} & 1 & 10 \\ 0 & \boxed{-5} & -15 \end{array}\right] $$

Multiplier $12 / 1 = 12$. Subtract 12 × the budget rule from the return rule: $-5y = -15$.

So $y = 3$, then $x = 7$: ₹7 lakh Nifty, ₹3 lakh bonds.

What changed?

New line, same answer

Elimination swaps the return line for $y = 3$. The crossing point $(7, 3)$ doesn't move.

3 × 3 · column 1

Clear below the first pivot

$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 10 \\ 12 & 7 & 15 & 111 \\ 1 & 0 & 1.2 & 7.4 \end{array}\right] $$
  • $\ell_{21} = 12$: row 2 − 12 × row 1
  • $\ell_{31} = 1$: row 3 − row 1
$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} \boxed{1} & 1 & 1 & 10 \\ 0 & -5 & 3 & -9 \\ 0 & -1 & 0.2 & -2.6 \end{array}\right] $$
3 × 3 · column 2

Clear below the second pivot

$\ell_{32} = (-1)/(-5) = 0.2$: row 3 − 0.2 × row 2.

$$ [U \mid c] = \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} \boxed{1} & 1 & 1 & 10 \\ 0 & \boxed{-5} & 3 & -9 \\ 0 & 0 & \boxed{-0.4} & -0.8 \end{array}\right] $$
Result

$A$ became upper triangular $U$; $b$ became $c$. Same solution. Pivots $1, -5, -0.4$ on the diagonal.

Back substitution

Solve from the bottom up

  1. $-0.4z = -0.8 \Rightarrow z = 2$
  2. $-5y + 3(2) = -9 \Rightarrow y = 3$
  3. $x + 3 + 2 = 10 \Rightarrow x = 5$

₹5 lakh Nifty, ₹3 lakh bonds, ₹2 lakh midcap.

The whole method

Forward elimination, then back substitution. That's all a computer does, with millions of rows.

The row picture

After elimination

New planes, ending with the flat $z = 2$. They still meet at the same point $(5, 3, 2)$.

When it breaks

A zero in the pivot position

Temporary: a non-zero entry below it? Exchange rows and carry on. (A credit fund earning 12% like Nifty causes this.)

Permanent: nothing non-zero below? No pivot: the matrix is singular. (A 50/50 balanced fund: the last row becomes $0 = -0.8$.)

Pivots decide everything

$n \times n$ matrix: $n$ pivots ⇔ invertible. Fewer: no solution or infinitely many.

Steps as matrices

Each step is a multiplication

Row × matrix combines rows: $\begin{bmatrix} -12 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} A = -12\,(\text{row }1) + (\text{row }2)$.

$$ E_{21} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -12 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \qquad E_{32}E_{31}E_{21}A = U $$

Parentheses can move (associative), so $E = E_{32}E_{31}E_{21}$ does all of elimination at once.

But order matters

$AB \ne BA$ in general.

Two more tools

Swaps and undo

Permutation $P$: the identity with rows swapped. $PA$ swaps rows of $A$; $AP$ swaps columns.

$$ E_{21}^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 12 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \quad \text{adds back what } E_{21} \text{ subtracted} $$
Why it works

Every step can be undone, so $Ax = b$ and $Ux = c$ have exactly the same solutions.

Recap

Key takeaways

  1. Elimination turns $Ax = b$ into triangular $Ux = c$, same solution.
  2. Multiplier = entry ÷ pivot. Pivots can't be 0.
  3. Back substitution: last row first.
  4. Zero pivot: swap rows, or the matrix is singular.
  5. Each step is a matrix: $E_{32}E_{31}E_{21}A = U$.
  6. Steps are reversible, which is why it's right.
