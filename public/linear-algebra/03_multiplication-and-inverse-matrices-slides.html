Multiplication and Inverse Matrices

Five ways to see a matrix product. Then the inverse: when it exists, and how Gauss–Jordan finds it.

Shown on real money questions: many portfolios, hedges and option payoffs.

AB five waysGauss–JordanMIT 18.06, Lecture 3
The setup

Many clients, one set of funds

Stack each client's portfolio as a column of $X$. One product gives every client's budget and return:

$$ \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 7 \end{bmatrix}}_{A:\ \text{funds}}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 7 & 4 \\ 3 & 6 \end{bmatrix}}_{X:\ \text{clients}} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 & 10 \\ 105 & 90 \end{bmatrix} $$

Then the reverse question: given the targets, which portfolios? That's the inverse.

Way 1

Row times column

Entry $(i, j)$ = row $i$ of $A$ · column $j$ of $B$:

$$ c_{22} = 12 \times 4 + 7 \times 6 = 90 $$

Client 2's return: ₹4 lakh Nifty earns 48, ₹6 lakh bonds earns 42.

Rule · Shapes

$(m \times n)(n \times p) = m \times p$. Inner sizes must match.

Ways 2 and 3

By columns, by rows

Columns of $AB$ combine columns of $A$: one column per client.

$$ A\begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} = 7\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix} + 3\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ 105 \end{bmatrix} $$

Rows of $AB$ combine rows of $B$: one row per rule.

$$ 12\,(7, 4) + 7\,(3, 6) = (105, 90) $$
Way 4

Columns times rows

A column times a row is a whole matrix. $AB$ is a sum of them, one per fund:

$$ \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 7 & 4 \\ 84 & 48 \end{bmatrix}}_{\text{Nifty's part}} + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 6 \\ 21 & 42 \end{bmatrix}}_{\text{bonds' part}} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 & 10 \\ 105 & 90 \end{bmatrix} $$
In markets

Attribution by source: what each fund contributes to every client.

Way 5

Block multiplication

Cut the matrices into blocks that match, then multiply blocks like numbers:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} A_1 & A_2 \\ A_3 & A_4 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} B_1 & B_2 \\ B_3 & B_4 \end{bmatrix} \;\to\; \text{top-left} = A_1B_1 + A_2B_3 $$

Three funds: "Nifty + bonds" part plus "midcap" part. Big computers multiply this way.

Inverses

When can A be undone?

$A^{-1}A = I = AA^{-1}$. But two identical Nifty funds:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 12 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

₹1 lakh more in one, ₹1 lakh less in the other: a trade that changes nothing.

Rule · Singular test

$Ax = 0$ with $x \ne 0$ ⇒ no inverse.

Gauss–Jordan · 2 × 2

[A | I] → [I | A⁻¹]

$$ \left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 12 & 7 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right] \to \left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -5 & -12 & 1 \end{array}\right] $$

Gauss goes down. Jordan goes up, then divide by the pivots:

$$ \left[\begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 0 & -1.4 & 0.2 \\ 0 & 1 & 2.4 & -0.2 \end{array}\right] $$
Gauss–Jordan · 3 × 3

The three-fund portfolio

After the downward half, the right side is Lesson 2's elimination matrix $E$, messy 1.4 and all. Then up, then divide:

$$ A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 4.2 & -0.6 & 4 \\ 0.3 & 0.1 & -1.5 \\ -3.5 & 0.5 & -2.5 \end{bmatrix} $$
Why it works

$E[A \mid I] = [EA \mid E]$. When $EA = I$, $E$ must be $A^{-1}$.

Reading A⁻¹

Columns = building-block trades

ColumnTrade (Nifty, bonds, midcap)Effect
1$(4.2, 0.3, -3.5)$+₹1 lakh budget only
2$(-0.6, 0.1, 0.5)$+₹1k return only
3$(4, -1.5, -2.5)$+1 exposure only

Want ₹5k more return, nothing else changed? Trade $5 \times$ column 2.

Options

Arrow–Debreu building blocks

For futures + 19,900 call + 20,100 call, the columns of $A^{-1}$ give lot mixes that pay in exactly one expiry scenario:

  • Sell 1 futures, buy 2 × 20,100 C: pays 200 only at 19,800
  • Buy 1 × 19,900 C, sell 3 × 20,100 C: pays 100 only at 20,000
  • Buy 1 × 20,100 C: pays 100 only at 20,200

Any payoff over these three levels is a mix of these.

Recap

Key takeaways

  1. $AB$ by entries, columns, rows, column×row, or blocks: same answer.
  2. No inverse if some trade changes nothing ($Ax = 0$).
  3. Gauss–Jordan: $[A \mid I] \to [I \mid A^{-1}]$.
  4. $X = A^{-1}B$ solves every target at once.
  5. Columns of $A^{-1}$ are building blocks.
