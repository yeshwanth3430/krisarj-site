Solving Ax = 0

The recipe for finding every do-nothing trade, even when you have more funds than rules.

Pivots, free variables, rank, special solutions.

Rank rn − r special solutionsMIT 18.06, Lecture 7
The setup

Four funds, three rules

Nifty, a Nifty ETF (its twin), bonds, balanced (half Nifty, half bonds). Rows: budget, return, exposure.

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 12 & 7 & 9.5 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} $$

Which trades change nothing? Two jump out: swap Nifty for its twin, and build balanced from Nifty + bonds. Are there more? A four-move recipe answers for sure.

Move 1 · why it's safe

Elimination rewrites the rules

"Return − 12 × budget" asks: how much return beyond 12%? A trade answering 0 to both old questions answers $0 - 12 \times 0 = 0$ to the new one.

So $Ax = 0$ and $Ux = 0$ have the same solutions.

Careful

The column space does change. Use $U$ for the nullspace, never for $C(A)$.

Move 1

Simplify to a staircase

$$ U = \begin{bmatrix} \boxed{1} & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & \boxed{-5} & -2.5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Column 2 has no pivot (the ETF copies Nifty), so skip it. Row 3 vanishes: exposure added no new information.

Pivot columns: Nifty, bonds. Free columns: ETF, balanced. Rank $r = 2$.

Move 2

Pivot = forced, free = your choice

Choose the free amounts. The pivot amounts are then forced:

$$ x_3 = -0.5\,x_4 \qquad x_1 = -x_2 - 0.5\,x_4 $$
Rank

$r$ = number of pivots = number of genuinely different funds.

Move 3

Special solutions

One free variable = 1, the others 0:

  • ETF = 1: $s_1 = (-1, 1, 0, 0)$: guess 1
  • Balanced = 1: $s_2 = (-0.5, 0, -0.5, 1)$: guess 2

Check $s_2$: $-0.5 - 0.5 + 1 = 0$, $\;-6 - 3.5 + 9.5 = 0$, $\;-0.5 + 0.5 = 0$ ✓

Move 4: every do-nothing trade is $c\,s_1 + d\,s_2$, and nothing else exists: a plane in $\mathbb{R}^4$.

The rule

More funds than rules ($n > m$) ⇒ a do-nothing trade always exists.

The reduced form

R: clear above the pivots too

$$ R = \begin{bmatrix} \boxed{1} & 1 & 0 & 0.5 \\ 0 & 0 & \boxed{1} & 0.5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Pivot columns first: $R = [\,I \;\; F\,]$ with

$$ F = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0.5 \\ 0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} $$
Read it off

The nullspace matrix

$$ N = \begin{bmatrix} -F \\ I \end{bmatrix} = \begin{array}{c} x_1 \\ x_3 \\ x_2 \\ x_4 \end{array}\!\begin{bmatrix} -1 & -0.5 \\ 0 & -0.5 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Its columns are the special solutions, and $RN = I(-F) + F\,I = 0$. No back substitution needed.

In markets

Four products, three scenarios

Futures, 20,000 call, 20,000 put, 19,900 call. The recipe finds one free column (the put) and one special solution:

$$ (1, -1, 1, 0) $$

Long future, short call, long put pays 0 everywhere: put–call parity. The 19,900 call is in no do-nothing trade: it's the only one paying at 20,000.

Recap

Key takeaways

  1. Simplify: elimination keeps the do-nothing trades.
  2. Pivot amounts are forced, free ones chosen.
  3. Special solution: one free = 1, others 0.
  4. All of them: mixes of the special solutions.
  5. $n - r$ of them; $n > m$ ⇒ always one.
  6. $R = [\,I \;\; F\,]$, $N = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} -F \\ I \end{smallmatrix}\right]$.
