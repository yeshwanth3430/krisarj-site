Linear Algebra · Lesson 7

Solving Ax = 0

Which trades change nothing? Simplify $A \to U \to R$, then read every answer off $R$.

Rank, pivot and free variables, special solutions.

A → U → Rn − r freeMIT 18.06, Lecture 7
The question

Four funds, three rules

Nifty, Nifty ETF (its twin), bonds, balanced (half Nifty, half bonds). Rows: budget, return, exposure.

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 12 & 7 & 9.5 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} $$

Two do-nothing trades jump out: swap Nifty for its twin, and build balanced from Nifty + bonds. Are there more?

Step 1 · A → U

Clear below the pivots

$$ U = \begin{bmatrix} \boxed{1} & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & \boxed{-5} & -2.5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Row 2 − 12 × row 1 asks: how much return beyond 12%? Row 3 ends up all zeros: exposure told us nothing new.

Safe: a trade that answers 0 to the old rules answers 0 to the new ones.

Step 2 · U → R

Pivots to 1, clear above

Row 2 ÷ (−5), then row 1 − row 2:

$$ R = \begin{bmatrix} \boxed{1} & 1 & 0 & 0.5 \\ 0 & 0 & \boxed{1} & 0.5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Row 1: $x_1 + x_2 + 0.5x_4 = 0$. Row 2: $x_3 + 0.5x_4 = 0$. Each rule now involves one pivot amount only.

The rank

r = number of pivots = 2

  • 2 of 3 rules carry information
  • 2 of 4 funds bring something new (Nifty, bonds)
  • 4 − 2 = 2 amounts are left free
Pivot and free

You pick, R decides

Free (no pivot): ETF, balanced. Pivot: Nifty, bonds.

Pick ETF = 3, balanced = 2. Read $R$:

bonds $= -0.5(2) = -1$,   Nifty $= -3 - 0.5(2) = -4$

Common sense agrees

Undo the ETF by selling 3 Nifty; undo balanced by selling 1 Nifty + 1 bonds.

A free column ⇒ yes. More funds than rules ⇒ always yes.

Special solutions

Read them off R

One free amount = 1, the other 0. Pivot amounts = minus that column of $R$:

  • ETF = 1: column $(1, 0)$ → $s_1 = (-1, 1, 0, 0)$: guess 1
  • Balanced = 1: column $(0.5, 0.5)$ → $s_2 = (-0.5, 0, -0.5, 1)$: guess 2

Every do-nothing trade is $c\,s_1 + d\,s_2$, and nothing else exists.

In markets

Four products, three scenarios

Futures, 20,000 call, 20,000 put, 19,900 call. $R$ has one free column (the put) and one special solution:

$$ (1, -1, 1, 0) $$

Long future, short call, long put pays 0 everywhere: put–call parity.

Recap

Key takeaways

  1. $A \to U \to R$ keeps the do-nothing trades.
  2. Rank $r$ = number of pivots.
  3. Pivot amounts are decided by $R$; free ones you choose.
  4. Free column ⇒ non-zero solutions; none ⇒ only 0.
  5. Special solution: one free = 1, pivots = −column of $R$.
  6. All solutions: mixes of the special solutions.
