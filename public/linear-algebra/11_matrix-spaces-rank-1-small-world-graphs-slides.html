Matrix Spaces; Rank 1; Small World Graphs

Matrices can be vectors too. A covariance matrix lives in one such space, and a single-factor market is a rank one matrix.

dim S = n(n+1)/2A = uvᵀMIT 18.06, Lecture 11
Matrix spaces

Anything you can add and scale

All 3 × 3 matrices form a space $M$ of dimension 9 (basis: 9 matrices with a single 1).

SubspaceDimension
Symmetric $S$6
Upper triangular $U$6
Diagonal $D = S \cap U$3

$\dim S + \dim U = \dim(S + U) + \dim(S \cap U)$: $6 + 6 = 9 + 3$.

In markets

Covariance lives in S

A covariance matrix is symmetric. For $n$ assets you need

$$ \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \text{ numbers} $$
  • 2 assets: 3 (two variances, one covariance)
  • 3 assets: 6
  • 50 stocks: 1,275

Diagonal covariance = uncorrelated assets.

Rank one

A column times a row

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1.2 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} -10 & 5 & 20 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -10 & 5 & 20 \\ -12 & 6 & 24 \\ -5 & 2.5 & 10 \end{bmatrix} $$

Betas × market returns: every row a multiple of one row. One factor drives everything ⇒ rank 1.

Building blocks

Rank r = r rank-one pieces

Add a small-cap factor to midcap: $+ (0, 1, 0)^T(4, -2, 6)$. Midcap's row becomes $(-8, 4, 30)$, no longer a multiple of Nifty's.

Two factors ⇒ rank 2.

Rank 1 + rank 1 is usually rank 2, so rank-one matrices are not a subspace.

The market model

Cov = σ²ββᵀ + D

$$ \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 400 & 480 & 0 \\ 480 & 576 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}}_{400\,\beta\beta^T \text{ (rank 1)}} + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 324 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 25 \end{bmatrix}}_{D} $$ $$ = \begin{bmatrix} 400 & 480 & 0 \\ 480 & 900 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 25 \end{bmatrix} $$
50 stocks

Full covariance: 1,275 numbers. One factor: 50 + 1 + 50 = 101.

Small world graphs

How far apart are two brokers?

Nodes = brokers, edges = direct trading links. Distance = fewest links between two nodes.

The adjacency matrix $A$ has a 1 for each link; $(A^2)_{ij}$ counts two-step paths.

In finance networks, distance is how many hops a shock takes to spread.

Recap

Key takeaways

  1. Matrices form vector spaces: 3 × 3 has dim 9.
  2. Symmetric: $n(n+1)/2$, where covariance lives.
  3. Rank one $= uv^T$: a single-factor model.
  4. Rank $r$ = $r$ rank-one pieces = $r$ factors.
  5. Graphs: distance, adjacency, $A^2$.
